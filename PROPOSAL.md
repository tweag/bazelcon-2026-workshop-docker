# Building Without Bytes: High-Velocity Image Composition at Scale

## Abstract

This talk has a confession to make: the slides you are watching were not exported from a presentation app — they were *built* by Bazel, from the same workspace that builds the container images we are about to discuss. This is self-hosting taken to its logical conclusion. We use `rules_oci` and `rules_img` to assemble the container images in our examples, and we use Bazel to compile, test, and package the presentation itself as a content-addressable artifact. The thesis falls out naturally: if you can model something as fluid and "creative" as a conference talk as a deterministic build artifact, you can model your entire software supply chain the same way. The presentation is the proof of concept — and we will show you its source.

The core of the talk is a migration story: moving off the Docker daemon and the imperative `Dockerfile` to declarative, Bazel-native image construction. We will contrast the two worlds directly. The `Dockerfile` workflow treats layers as opaque binary blobs produced by a stateful daemon, where a one-character change near the top of the file invalidates everything below it, caching is coarse and host-dependent, and "build" and "test" are serialized through a single mutable engine. Against that, we demonstrate how `rules_oci` and `rules_img` produce layers as granular, individually cacheable build outputs — yielding genuinely incremental rebuilds, fine-grained remote layer caching keyed on content rather than instruction order, and image tests that fan out and run in parallel across Bazel's action graph. The result is faster feedback loops and reproducible images that don't depend on what happened to be cached on the build machine.

Attendees will leave with three concrete things. First, a **blueprint for a "Slide-as-Code" repository** — the actual structure that builds this very presentation, ready to clone and adapt. Second, a **benchmarking framework** for honestly comparing a traditional Docker build against an equivalent Bazel/OCI workflow, so you can measure the incremental-build and cache-hit wins on your own images instead of taking ours on faith. Third, and most durable, a **mental model**: stop thinking of container layers as opaque tarballs handed to you by a daemon, and start treating them as granular, deterministic, cacheable build outputs like any other target in your graph. Once layers are just artifacts, the daemon stops being special — and your container builds inherit every guarantee Bazel already gives the rest of your code.
